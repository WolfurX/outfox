# The Open Market, Desks, and Seats

The long game on the Street is economic, and most of it runs through markets where other players set the prices.

## The Open Market

The item market: terminals, feeds, models, and the jacket-color cosmetic line. Listings are player-to-player with floating prices; the game does not fix them. Trading here is live in the current build.

## Desks **\[designed]**

Player-run businesses: prop desks, OTC desks, research desks. Desks are where specialization pays. A Fox who is better at one thing than everyone else earns from the difference, which is what makes the economy thick instead of a row of parallel grinders.

## Seats **\[designed]**

A Seat on the Exchange, the Street's most conspicuous possession. Historically real, and a deliberate piece of economic machinery: Seats are status goods whose demand rises with their price, which makes them a drain on large fortunes that never touches gameplay power. See [Two currencies](../the-economy/two-currencies.md) for why the economy wants that.

## The Index **\[designed]**

The Street's internal market, with visibly fictional tickers. The Index gives late-game capital something to do that is competitive, risky, and entirely inside the game's walls.

***

A note on what "**\[designed]**" means here: validated in the economic model, not yet shipped as code. [What exists today](../status/what-exists-today.md) keeps the full list honest.
