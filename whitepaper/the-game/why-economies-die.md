# Why game economies die

GameFi mortality sits around 90 to 93%, and the deaths rhyme. A token printer funds player earnings. The faucet outruns the sinks. Supply inflates, price falls, earners leave, and the thing unwinds. Axie Infinity and StepN are the famous cases.

The failure mode is not "crypto." It is **emission funding**. If the game mints the token that players earn, player earnings are a claim written against future buyers. When the buyers stop, the claim breaks. That is arithmetic, not sentiment, and no amount of tokenomics cleverness repairs it.

## The counterexample

**Torn**, a text-based crime MMO with a deep player economy, has run since 2004 with players who explicitly play to earn, and it does not collapse. The reason is unglamorous: it is **transfer-funded**. Players earn from other players, plus real-money convenience spending. No token is printed.

Outfox copies that structure and refuses the reflex to bolt a token faucet onto it.

## The invariant, stated precisely

$ALPHA is **never minted to players**. Player earnings come from player-to-player transfer, from real-money convenience spend, and from Scrip faucets whose sinks are calibrated to outrun them. There is no emission faucet paying free tokens for showing up.

## About "no faucet", the precise claim

You will see projects claim they have "no faucet." Here is exactly what we claim, and what we don't.

**Scrip has faucets.** Gigs and Calls create it; saying otherwise would be false, and the code says otherwise. What we claim is narrower and checkable: Scrip's sinks are calibrated to slightly outrun its faucets at steady state (the validated designed-only ratio is 0.984), and **$ALPHA is never minted to players at all**. There is no "log in, collect free tokens" mechanic, and there cannot be: the token has no minting path.

That distinction is the entire thesis. The rest of this document is what it takes to make it hold when real people show up to farm it.
